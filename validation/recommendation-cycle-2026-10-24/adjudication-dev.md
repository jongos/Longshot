## Evidence and differences

- **D1:** The packet says editors may save answers and only owners may alter checklists. Baseline, scope and preservation explicitly test that boundary. Cause retains “owner restrictions” but leaves “Editor-only behavior ... untested” without putting editor answer-saving in acceptance. That weakens preservation coverage; it does not recommend removing it. Scope's local cue, “repeating 'Project answers' beside its distant Save,” is an equally supported alternative to relabeling both buttons, not a proven improvement over baseline.
- **D2:** All correctly leave receipt unresolved and reject a retry. Scope's “then outcome feedback” is less clearly conditional on finding a product defect than baseline's “no ... redesign is justified”; a diagnostic can instead vindicate existing feedback. Cause usefully specifies holding date/session/fixture constant, but “failure only with interception points toward tooling” is suggestive, not isolation of tooling versus an application/interception interaction. Its later acceptance of “every attempt” having truthful status is broader than a bounded check, and does not explicitly require correlated receipt evidence as baseline does. Preservation asks for a matched trace “with interception disabled” to “isolate interception”; one new disabled trace is insufficient without a matched enabled control, and correlation alone does not guarantee a terminal outcome. These are future proposals; all reports honor the supplied-traces-only limit. Cause uniquely states precisely why the permitted lookup is unavailable: “no request ID.”
- **D3:** All retain the demonstrated safeguard and reject removal for step count alone. The packet requires explicit confirmation and supports Cancel/target isolation. Incorrect-name tests are proposals, not invented executed evidence; baseline makes that distinction particularly explicit. Scope adds roles and refresh checks, useful possible coverage but not evidence of an existing problem. Removing this dialog without replacement would violate the requirement; the reports should not be read as proving every alternative explicit-confirmation design invalid.
- **D4:** All correctly use the visible entry point and keyboard trace to defeat an unsupported gallery-placement complaint. Scope's proposed check retains keyboard activation but omits verification of the supported focus return, replacing that specificity with previously unknown saved-template/unrelated-note mutation checks. Preservation adds the useful distinction “Shortcut execution itself is not established by its label.” Its populated-note acceptance says it “must protect existing work through explicit replacement consent or another non-destructive route.” This is a reasonable proposed safety criterion, but is newly introduced product policy outside the empty-note task; it must not become a packet-established defect or implementation requirement. Baseline and cause keep the current task and focus check most tightly bounded.
- **D5:** Every variant supports a noncommitting comparison route from “details appear only after selecting Apply” and two traces of unrecovered title replacement. High severity is defensible as an evaluative judgment but remains unmeasured, especially given the saved-copy workaround. Scope usefully separates read-only inspection from “establish intended title policy before changing Apply.” Cause adds the clearest discriminating contract check and alternative remedies; this is useful reasoning, though a non-mutating details route need not wait for resolution of Apply's title contract. Preservation's “continuity defect” is stronger wording than baseline's “concern,” but the observed task disruption supports it; it does not establish an implementation bug. Its requirement that title replacement have disclosed intent and recovery is a proposed remedy criterion, not a supplied contract. No material regression here.
- **D6:** All preserve exact frames and avoid assuming that seconds should be preserved automatically. Scope's additional unknown role/assets scope offers little task-specific value. Cause usefully separates arithmetic correctness from policy and notes rounding if a time-authoritative policy were later selected. Preservation adds directly useful current-fps context and arbitrary-integer acceptance. However, “Any policy change needs an explicit choice” is stronger than the packet: it requires a product decision, but does not establish that the end user must choose the policy. Treat it as a design suggestion, not a prerequisite for every future policy change.

## Decision

The baseline already handles all six packets well. No variant demonstrates a broad enough improvement to justify wholesale replacement or a mandatory new checklist across reports. The most useful incremental additions are cause's D2 authorization/lookup precision and D5 competing-contract reasoning, plus preservation's D4 shortcut-evidence distinction and D6 exact-integer/current-fps checks. Scope improves D5 operation separation but also adds low-value unknown-scope inventory elsewhere.

The clearest regressions are reduced preservation/test specificity in cause D1 and scope D4; preservation also risks turning reasonable adjacent safeguards into unrequested requirements. D2 diagnostic wording needs tightening before reuse. These are reporting regressions, not observed product regressions.

None of the variants deserves blanket implementation on this synthetic comparison alone. Selective, narrowly worded reporting improvements are supportable; no source, skill, or product change was made by this adjudication. D3 and D4 warrant preserving the demonstrated behavior, D2 warrants an evidence gap, and only D1/D5/D6 support bounded design proposals from these packets.
