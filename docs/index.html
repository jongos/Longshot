01 / The method

A Skeptic With a Test Plan.

Longshot works like an independent tester: curious about the intent, unconvinced by the presentation, and willing to change its mind.

It challenges workflows that get in the way and reports what works, with evidence for each conclusion.

Evidence Before Opinion

Separate observed defects, plausible usability concerns, aesthetic preferences and missing evidence.

Useful Recommendations

Explain the obstacle and provide checks for both the improvement and the useful behavior that must survive it. “Make it intuitive” is not a fix you can verify.

A closer look / The protocol

Discover First.
Diagnose Second.

Knowing how the app works can hide how hard it is to use.

The First Encounter

Try the task through the interface, without source-code shortcuts or a designer's prescribed route. Record what consequential controls suggest before acting. Disclose prior knowledge when a fresh encounter is no longer possible.

The Diagnostic Pass

Then use authorized deeper evidence to reproduce and explain what happened. Completion with privileged help stays separate from unassisted discovery.

Real Conditions, Not Invented People

Choose relevant conditions: returning to a draft, comparing options, keyboard-only use, narrow screens, zoom, interruption or incomplete information.

Check What Happens After “Done”

Find the result. Return to it. Correct a mistake or recover work where authorized. Record completed, blocked, uncertain and untested states, plus any backtracking or help needed. A toast alone does not prove the job is finished.

02 / Put it to work

Test the Journey.
Not Just the Screen.

01

Before You Ship

Attempt onboarding, search, form completion or a core workflow. Find dead ends that a beautiful happy-path demo can miss.

02

When Work Gets Interrupted

Leave, return, cancel, retry. Check whether drafts, choices and context survive the routes people may need to take.

03

When “Done” Is Ambiguous

Distinguish a queued operation, a success message and a verified result. Investigate recovery without blindly resubmitting.

04

When the Default Path Excludes Someone

Exercise relevant keyboard, focus, zoom and responsive states. Find obstacles across the ways people need to use the interface.

05

When Everyone Agrees Too Quickly

Ask for an independent challenge to a designer's assumptions. Preserve useful expert complexity; question friction that serves no demonstrated purpose.

03 / How the loops work

Repeat to Learn.
Stop When the Evidence Ends.

1

Attempt

Choose a goal and discover the route before consulting implementation details.

2

Observe

Record expectations, outcomes and assistance. Keep each evidence source clear.

3

Challenge

Try an authorized alternative, interruption or competing explanation.

4

Verify

Check the result and relevant aftermath. Reproduce consequential findings within scope.

Each review stays focused on your task. Findings connect what happened to a change you can assess and a test you can repeat.

Reviews stay within your scope and access permissions. After a fix, retest the same task conditions and check for regressions.

04 / The deliverable

A Report You Can Act On.

COMPLETION

Did the task complete? Record assistance, prior knowledge and untested states.

REPLAY

Starting state → actions → expected and observed outcomes → screenshots or traces, when available.

JUDGMENT

Classification, severity, confidence and competing explanations

NEXT MOVE

The smallest useful change, safeguards to preserve, tradeoffs and a test that could disprove the recommendation.

A blocked task or lost work stays visible. An average score cannot make it disappear.

Evidence That Survives the Next Run

Track findings across reviews with stable IDs. Longshot retests the original problem before marking it resolved and shows which findings recurred, remained blocked or were not retested. Exported reports retain the same findings and coverage.

Field guide / Your direction

Leave the Rest to Longshot.

Automatic by default. As guided as you want.

Longshot can choose the important journeys itself. Or tell it who uses your product, what they need to do and what matters most. Use plain language. One sentence is enough to focus a review.

Start here / No brief required

Let Longshot Choose

Use $longshot-human-centered-ui to test this app using the supplied test account. Choose the important workflows. Report obstacles, evidence and recommendations. Do not modify the app.

It selects relevant tasks, states its assumptions and gets to work. It asks only when missing information changes access or the meaning of success.

Four Ways to Focus the Review

Use any combination. These are optional directions, not a checklist you must fill out.

01

Roles

Who is doing the work? Describe responsibilities, experience and what each role can see or do.

“Editors create drafts. Reviewers approve or return them but cannot edit the text.”

Use the corresponding test accounts. Naming a role does not grant its permissions.

02

Workflows

Describe the starting situation, goal and important branches. For handoffs, name who acts next and what they need to see.

“Submit a draft, return it with notes as a reviewer, then resume it as the editor.”

Give a goal to test discovery. Give exact clicks when you want a specific route checked.

03

Priorities

Tell Longshot where to spend attention, what comes first and what to leave out.

“Preserve work first. Check clear ownership next, then keyboard access. Skip billing.”

A focused journey is enough. You do not need to request a full audit.

04

Success Criteria

Describe a result that can be checked. Replace “make it intuitive” with a concrete outcome.

“After returning from the reference, the title and body still match what was entered.”

Unsure what to measure? Ask Longshot to propose success criteria from your goal.

Use a Complete Guided Brief

Copy this example and replace the roles and journey with your own.

Use $longshot-human-centered-ui to review our staging app.

Roles: Editors create and revise drafts. Reviewers approve or return drafts with notes; they cannot edit the text.
Workflows: Create a draft, consult the reference, return and submit. Return it for edits as a reviewer, then resume it as the editor.
Priorities: Preserve work first, clear ownership second, keyboard access third. Skip billing and administration.
Success: Text survives the reference trip. The reviewer sees the submitted version. The editor can find the return notes and resume the same draft without losing text.
Access: Use only supplied staging accounts and synthetic records.

Report evidence, working behavior, obstacles and acceptance tests. Do not modify the app.

Give Just the Part You Know

Role only: “Test as a first-time editor; choose the workflows.”

Workflow only: “Test creating and resuming a draft; infer the role.”

Priority only: “Choose the tasks, but focus on interruption recovery.”

Longshot fills the gaps and labels its assumptions. Change focus anytime: “Keep the editor role, test only resuming drafts, and skip submission.”
